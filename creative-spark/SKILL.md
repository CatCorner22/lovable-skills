# Creative spark

The first idea is the one every other app already has. Go past it on purpose. Then build the new idea with care, so it works as well as it looks.

## 1. Find the truth of the subject
Write 5 lines before any idea:
- Who uses this, and what do they feel just before they open it?
- The 1 job the app does for them.
- What is specific to this subject: its objects, places, sounds, textures, words, rituals.
- What every competitor does. List 5 things. These are the defaults to beat.
- Fixed limits: brand, colors, fonts, design system, audience needs. Respect them.

If the user answered Lovable's design questions, treat those picks as fixed and be creative inside them.

## 2. Diverge. Use subagents.
Start 4 read-only subagents at the same time. Give each the 5 lines and 1 lens. Each returns 1 concept: a name, 1 sentence, the signature moment, and what it costs to build.
- **Subject lens:** build the concept from the subject's own world. A bakery app borrows from paper bags and chalk, not from other apps.
- **Outside lens:** borrow from a far field: print, film, games, maps, museums, music gear, architecture.
- **Flip lens:** take the 5 competitor defaults and reverse 1. Remove the dashboard. Start with the result. Make the form a conversation.
- **Motion lens:** design how it moves and responds first. What changes as the user acts, scrolls, waits, or returns?

If no subagents start, write the 4 concepts yourself, one lens at a time. Reject any concept that could describe a different app with the name swapped.

Use [references/idea-moves.md](references/idea-moves.md) when a lens stalls.

## 3. Choose
Score each concept 1–5 on: fits the user, fresh for this category, buildable now, and still clear at a glance. Show the user the 4 concepts in 1 short table with your pick and the reason. Ask them to choose. If they said to decide, pick the highest score and say so.

For a large or risky change, show the top 2 as quick previews in a draft before committing.

## 4. Build 1 signature idea
Spend boldness in 1 place. Keep everything else calm and familiar.
- Name the signature: 1 moment, interaction, layout, or feature the user will remember and describe to a friend.
- Build it first, as its own small slice. Use real content, never placeholder text.
- Make it dynamic: it responds to the user's own data, action, time, or place. Static decoration does not count.
- Set the type, color, and spacing tokens to serve the concept. Define them once as variables.
- Keep navigation, forms, and core actions where users expect them.

Skip the worn defaults unless the concept truly needs them: purple-to-blue gradients, glass cards, glowing buttons, blurred orbs, a 3-card feature row, sparkle icons, vanity numbers, "AI-powered" claims, centered hero with 2 buttons.

## 5. Keep it usable
Check the rendered preview at 1440 px and 390 px against [references/craft-floor.md](references/craft-floor.md). A creative idea that fails this floor is not done. Fix it or cut it.

## 6. Report
Tell the user: the concept in 1 sentence, the signature idea, what you kept plain on purpose, what you checked, and 1 bolder step they can take next.
